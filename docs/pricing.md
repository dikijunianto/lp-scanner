# Timestamped USD pricing

The scanner stores independent price observations in `price_observations`, separate from pool snapshots. Every observation has the asset address, chain, symbol, USD price, provider, provider publication timestamp when supplied, local fetch time, optional block number, and confidence. Existing snapshots are not rewritten. GeckoTerminal and Meteora discovery prices are also recorded, with `sourceTimestamp = null`; their fetch time is not misrepresented as publication time.

The primary independent source is [DefiLlama Coins API](https://github.com/DefiLlama/api-sdk), queried by chain and contract address. Its response contains a price timestamp. A bounded CoinGecko token-price check is attempted for four stale or absent assets per protocol scan; [CoinGecko's token price endpoint](https://docs.coingecko.com/reference/simple-token-price) supplies `last_updated_at`. This fallback is best effort because the public endpoint can rate limit. Neither provider guarantees that a thin asset's price excludes trades from the pool under inspection; the scanner therefore checks the provider price against the separately discovered price and the on-chain pool ratio. It never averages conflicting quotes.

Default publication age at scan time: HIGH ≤120 seconds, MEDIUM ≤600 seconds, LOW ≤1,800 seconds, UNAVAILABLE after that. USDC/USDT prices in the $0.99–$1.01 range, independently corroborated by the discovery quote in the same range, may remain MEDIUM for up to 900 seconds; this configurable exception is visible through the recorded publication time and does not assume a $1 price. Missing publication time is LOW even when just fetched. These limits and the 10% disagreement warning / 30% rejection are configurable. A price disagreement lowers confidence and adds `PRICE_DISAGREEMENT`; severe disagreement makes that asset's USD valuation unavailable. LOW and UNAVAILABLE prices cannot produce active-liquidity or bounded-depth USD metrics. `PRICE_STALE` indicates a LOW price. On-chain block time and price publication time are kept separately.

For event valuation, the scanner looks up a price observation published near the event timestamp. If none exists, it requests the provider's historical price for that five-minute bucket and stores the returned publication timestamp. An event with no suitable price remains unvalued; a fee window containing it is unavailable. Price records from a different time are not silently carried forward as current spot prices. This is price-time alignment, not a claim that the oracle price was observed in the same EVM block. Most current-price API records have no block number; on-chain reads retain their block/slot in the liquidity metadata.
