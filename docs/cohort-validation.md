# Descriptive signal cohorts

Cohort version `sprint5-v1` aggregates the latest 5,000 signal episodes. The background worker persists aggregates after new outcomes. Primary signals: activity surge, high fee efficiency, high volume/depth, and low-risk high-activity. Group dimensions: chain, protocol, trend, risk, activity, volatility, data confidence, token age, and pool age. These are one-dimensional groups per signal type, plus an all-values group; they are not every possible joint combination.

Default research filter includes HIGH and MEDIUM confidence. HIGH-only and ALL are available. Each cohort shows its confidence composition and count of complete outcomes at 30 minutes, 1 hour, 4 hours, and 24 hours. The default minimum is `COHORT_MIN_SAMPLE=30`. Each displayed statistic requires 30 non-null observations at its own horizon. Lower counts show `INSUFFICIENT SAMPLE`. Median, p25 and p75 use sorted finite observations with linear interpolation. The persisted aggregate includes these robust statistics for activity persistence/ratio, fees, volume, adverse and favorable moves, liquidity and depth change, and observed range time.

Confidence uses the evidence needed by each signal: fee and active-liquidity confidence for high fee efficiency, price and depth for high volume/depth, fee confidence for activity surge, and overall data quality for low-risk high-activity. Thus missing unrelated depth does not hide a fee signal. Each outcome metric still has its own non-null sample gate; signal confidence does not claim that later prices or fee generation are known.

Historical range survival is the fraction of complete, observed outcomes that remained inside ±2.5%, ±5%, or ±10% until each horizon. This is a survival-style descriptive curve, not a prospective survival probability. Observed range duration is capped by the horizon for still-in-range observations; censoring and missing observations limit interpretation. Signal decay uses endpoint activity divided by activity at signal creation. Older outcome rows lack this new field and remain null; they are never silently rewritten. A chart starts at 1.0 at signal time and shows only statistically supported later points.

Episode creation, observation gaps, missing prices, overlapping pools, repeated regimes and selection bias can distort cohorts. No winner score, projected return, best pool or strategy recommendation is produced.
