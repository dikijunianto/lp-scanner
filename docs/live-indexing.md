# Live EVM fee indexing

Sprint 6 stores `live_fee_cursors` separately from the older `fee_cursors`. Live jobs read confirmed blocks near chain head first. Historical jobs keep their own contiguous cursor and never determine whether a current fee window is fresh. Both insert swaps idempotently by pool, transaction hash, and log index; a live insert does not overwrite an already priced historical event. A complete current window requires contiguous live time coverage and every swap in the window to have a reliable USD fee. Zero swaps is valid only after a successful complete log read.

`BASE_LIVE_BOOTSTRAP_BLOCKS`, `BSC_LIVE_BOOTSTRAP_BLOCKS`, `LIVE_MAX_BLOCKS_PER_JOB`, and `LIVE_POOLS_PER_CYCLE` bound catch-up work. A stale cursor or changed block hash is reset and replayed from recent confirmed history. The diagnostic page shows each cursor's last observed head, block lag, time lag, last successful log source, and freshness. Its head is the last observed head, not a continuously polled current head. Source latency is RPC response latency; historical coverage stays unknown until requested ranges have been read. `FRESH` is under two minutes, `DELAYED` two to ten minutes, and `STALE` beyond ten minutes. Pool freshness can be queried at `/api/diagnostics/pool-freshness/:id`.

`BSC_LOG_RPC_URLS` can provide dedicated log-capable HTTPS RPCs, while `BNB_RPC_URLS` remains supported. The read-only router probes actual `eth_getLogs` support and fails over with a circuit breaker. When a second healthy log-capable provider exists, the first range is reconciled by transaction hash and log index; differing block, address, topics or data causes `LOG_SOURCE_DISAGREEMENT` and the range is not accepted. If no second source is available, source confidence is limited to the primary provider. No indexer or subgraph response is treated as equivalent to on-chain logs without validation. [BNB Chain says its listed public mainnet RPCs disable `eth_getLogs`](https://docs.bnbchain.org/bnb-smart-chain/developers/json_rpc/json-rpc-endpoint/); a capable third-party or self-hosted source is necessary for dependable BSC logs.
